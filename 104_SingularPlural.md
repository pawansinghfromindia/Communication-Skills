**Singular and Plural**

## Level 1 - Foundation

<details>
  <summary> <b> 1. What is singular? </b> </summary>

<br/>

> **Singular means one**

Singular means **one person, one place, one thing, one animal, or one idea**.

| Singular noun            |
| ------------------------ |
| **boy**, means one boy   |
| **book**, means one book |
| **city**, means one city |
| **dog**, means one dog   |
| **idea**, means one idea |


Examples:
```
I have a book.
She has a dog.
Rahul is a student.
```


</details>
<!------------------------->


<details>
  <summary> <b> 2. What is plural? </b> </summary>

<br/>

> Plural means **more than one**.

| Singular | Plural |
| -------- | ------ |
| boy      | boys   |
| book     | books  |
| city     | cities |
| dog      | dogs   |
| idea     | ideas  |


Examples:
```
I have two books.
She has three dogs.
They are students.
```

</details>
<!------------------------->


<details>
  <summary> <b> 3. Why is singular/plural important? </b> </summary>

<br/>

Singular/plural affect many parts of English grammar like `Noun`, `Pronoun`, and `Verb`

This is extremely important because **subject–verb agreement** depends on singular/plural.

```
        Singular        Plural
       one student  |  two students   
       a student    |  students
       an apple     | apples
   The boy plays.   | The boys play.
```

</details>
<!------------------------->

<details>
  <summary> <b> Conclusion : Foundations </b> </summary>

<br/>

Plural is not simply adding `-s/es`.

It is a system of patterns + exceptions + grammatical behavior.

```
                      NOUN NUMBER
                           │
              ┌────────────┴────────────┐
              │                         │
          SINGULAR                    PLURAL
            (one)                 (more than one)
              │                         │
       ┌──────┴──────┐           ┌──────┴─────────┐
       │             │           │                │
     Regular      Irregular     Regular        Irregular
       │             │            │                │
      book          man          books            men
      box           child        boxes            children
      city          tooth        cities           teeth
      knife         mouse        knives           mice
```


</details>

<details>
  <summary> <b> 3. Compound nouns plurals </b> open compounds, hyphenated compounds </summary>

<br/>

> Compound nouns contain **two or more words functioning together as a noun**.

Examples: `mother-in-law, brother-in-law, passer-by, toothbrush, school bus, police officer, etc`

The plurals rule depends on the structure.

1. **Open compounds** 

Examples: `school bus, police officer, tooth brush, etc`

We make plural of open compounds by pluralizing principal(main) noun.

|  Singular      |  Plural         |                   |
|----------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| school bus     | school buses    | ~schools bus~     |
| police officer | police officers | ~polices officer~ |

2. **Hyphenated compounds**

Examples: `mother-in-law, brother-in-law, passer-by, etc`

We make plural of hyphenated compounds by pluralizing principal(main) noun.

|  Singular          |  Plural             |                       |
|--------------------|---------------------|-----------------------|
| mother-in-law      | mothers-in-law      | ~mother-in-laws~      |            
| brother-in-law     | brothers-in-law     | ~brother-in-laws~     |
| passer-by          | passers-by          | ~passer-bys~          |
| commander-in-chief | commanders-in-chief | ~commander-in-chiefs~ |

<details>
  <summary> <b> 5. Subject Verb Agreement Rules </b> </summary>

<br/> 

The verb must agree with the subject in number (singular/plural) and, in some cases, person.

#### Basic Rule

| Singular subject --> singular verb |
|------------------------------------|
| **Plural subject --> plural verb** |

```
# Singular subject -> singular verb
- He works here.
- She plays cricket.
- Rahul speaks English.
- The dog runs fast.

# Plural subject -> plural verb
- They work here.
- We play cricket.
- Rahul and Amit speak English.
- The dogs run fast.

# Exception for I and You
- You work here.
- I play cricket.
```

| Subject                         |                  |               |
| --------------------------------|------------------|---------------|
| `I` (1st Person)                | Singular Subject | Plural Verb   |
| `We` (1st Person)               | Plural Subject   | Plural Verb   |
| `You` (2nd Person)              | Singular/Plural Subject | Plural Verb   |
| `He`/`She`/`It`/`Any Name` (3rd Person) | Singular Subject | Singular Verb |
| They    (3rd Person)            | Plural Subject   | Plural Verb   |

Note : `You` can refer to one person or multiple people, but it takes the same verb form.

**`s/es`**

`He/She/It/Any name` -> `verb + s/es` <br/>
`I/You/We/They`  -> `base verb`

**`Have/Has`**
| Subject | Verb |
| ------- | ---- |
| `I/We`  | have |
| `You`   | have |
| `He/She/It/Any Name` <br/> `They` | has <br/> have |

```
I have a car.
We have a car.
You have a car.
He/She/Ram has a car.
They have a car.
```

**`Do/Does`**
| Subject | Verb |
| ------- | ---- |
| `I/We`  | do   |
| `You`   | do   |
| `He/She/It/Any Name` <br/> `They` | does <br/> do |

```
I do my work.
We do our work.
You do your work.
He/She/Ram does his work.
They do their work.
```

**`be`** : It is especially important because its forms change significantly.
| Subject | Present  |
| ------- | -------- |
| `I`     | am/was   |
| `We`    | are/were |
| `You`   | are/were |
| `He/She/It/Any Name` | is/was |
| `They`  | are/were   |

```
I am ready.
I was busy.
We are ready.
You are ready.
He/She/Ram was busy.
They were busy.
```

#### Two subjects joined by `and`  

When two subjects are joined by and, the verb is usually form plural subject because the subject becomes plural.

`Subject + and + Subject -> plural verb`
```
Rahul and Amit are friends.
Tom and Jerry play together.
My friend and his father live in Mumbai.
```

#### Two subjects joined by `or/nor`

When subjects are joined by or or nor, the verb generally agrees with the subject closest to it.

`Subject + or/nor + closest subject -> verb agrees with the closest subject.`
```
Rahul or Amit is coming.
Rahul or his friends are coming.
```

#### Common singular indefinite subjects take singular verbs

Even though `each` or `every` may refer to many people/things, the verb is generally singular. <br/>
bcuz Each/Every is **one at a time** -> singular verb

```
Each student has a book.
Every student needs a laptop.
Each employee is responsible.
```

Common singular indefinite subjects : `everyone, everybody, someone, somebody, anyone, anybody, no one, nobody, each, every`

```
Everyone is ready.
Everybody likes music.
Someone has called you.
Nobody knows the answer.
```

####  Uncountable nouns usually take singular verbs

Uncountable nouns : `information, advice, knowledge, furniture, money, water, rice, etc`

```
The information is useful.
The advice is helpful.
The furniture is expensive.
The money is missing.
```

#### Collective Nouns usually take singular verbs but sometimes plural

When the group is considered as one unit, a singular verb is commonly used. But It also depends on the context whether group acts as a single unit or as individual members.

Collective nouns : `team, family, committee, class, audience, company, government, jury, etc`

```
The team is ready.
The committee has made a decision.
The family is happy.
The company is growing.
---------------------------------------
The team is winning the match.              Singular (unit)
The team are arguing among themselves.      Plural (individuals)

The audience was silent.                    Singular (unit)
The audience were clapping and cheering.    Plural (individuals)

The jury has reached a verdict.             Singular (unit)
The jury are divided on the evidence.       Plural (individuals)

The class is taking a test                  Singular (unit)
The class are chatting during the exam.     Plural (individuals)

The family is having dinner together.        Singular (unit)
The family are all different personalities.  Plural (individuals)
```


#### `There is` vs `There are`

The real subject comes after the verb.
```
There is a book on the table.       Subject = a book -> singular
There are two books on the table.   Subject = two books -> plural
There is a problem.
There are several problems.
```

#### `A Number of` vs `The Number of`

A number of -> plural, The number of -> singular
```
A number of students are absent.
The number of students is increasing.
```

#### Amounts of time, money, distance take singular verbs

When an amount is considered as one unit, we often use a singular verb.

```
Ten dollars/rupees is enough.
Five years is a long time.
Ten kilometers is too far for me.
```

But when referring to individual units, plural bcuz Here, we're thinking about individual dollar bills/coins rather than one total amount.
```
Ten dollars were lying on the table.
```

#### One of + Plural Noun -> take singular verbs bcuz the subject is one, not plural.

```
One of my friends is a doctor.
One of the students has submitted the assignment.
```

#### Some nouns are always/usually plural

```
The scissors are on the table.
My trousers are new.
These clothes are expensive.
```

#### `as well as`, `along with`, `together with`

These expressions do not make the subject plural.
```
Rahul, along with his friends, is coming.       Main subject = Rahul -> singular.
Rahul and his friends are coming.               "and" makes it a compound subject.

The teacher, as well as the students, is attending the meeting.   Main subject = teacher -> singular.
```

### `few`, `many`, `several`, `some` and `both` take plural verbs

`few, many, several, some, and both` are always treated as plural and therefore take plural verbs.

```
Several  are absent.
Both the applicants are qualified.
Many of the students have completed the assignment.
Few people know the truth.
Several of the cars were damaged.
Some people in homeroom are very annoying.
```

#### Subjects ending in `-s` can still be singular

```
Mathematics is difficult.
Physics is interesting.
Economics is an important subject.
```

These subjects end in `s` but they're singular.

</details>

Each/Every are followed by singular countable nouns.

|          |          |
|----------|----------|

```

I like neither chocolate nor vanilla.
Neither of the answers is correct.
Neither the bus nor the train arrived on time.
She is neither tall nor short.
```

<details>
  <summary> <b> 1. Possessive singular/plural </b> </summary>

<br/>

**Singular possessive** <br/>
for singular possessiveness is describe by adding single apostrophe followed by `'s`
```
the boy's book    | Meaning: the book belonging to one boy
Ram's car
girl's frock
father's name
```

**Plural possessive** <br/>

**Regular plural possessive** <br/>
since plural already ends with `s` so possessiveness is describe by just adding single apostrophe`'` at the end:
```
boys' books
students' classroom
teachers' room
```

**Irregular plural possessive** <br/>
since plural doesn't ends with `s` so possessiveness is describe by adding single apostrophe followed by `'s`
```
children's toys
men's clothes
women's rights
```

| Possessiveness      |                                                             |                           |
|---------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------|
| Singular Possessive |   `'s`                                                      | boy's tshirt, man's right | 
| Plural Possessive   | Regular plural possessive <br/> Irregular plural possessive | `'` <br/> `'s` | boys' tshirts, students' book <br/> men's clothers, women's right |

<details>
  <summary> <b> 2. Collective nouns(groups) singular/plural </b> </summary>

<br/>

> **Treat a collective noun as singular when the group acts as one unit else plural.**

Examples: `team, family, committee, class, audience, government, staff, etc`

In standard **American English**, collective nouns are commonly treated as singular when the group is considered one unit.
```
The team is winning.
```

In **British English**, plural agreement is also possible when emphasizing the individual members.
```
The team are wearing their new uniforms.
```

|  Singular  |   Plural   |  Plural     |       
|------------|------------|-------------|
| team       | team       | teams       |
| family     | family     | families    |
| committee  | committee  | committees  |
| class      | class      | classes     |
| audience   | audience   | audiences   |
| staff      | staff      | staffs      |


**Public/Gathering** : crowd, mob, horde, throng, gathering, assembly, congregation, party (a group travelling together) 

**Legal/Decision-making** : jury, panel, bench (judges), quorum

**Military** : army, regiment, squadron, battalion, troop 

**Work/Organisation** : crew (sailors, firefighters), squad (police), board (directors), faculty (teachers), delegation (representatives), cabinet (ministers), union (workers), force (police/military

**Social/Informal** : clan, gang, circle, clique, coterie, posse

**Performance/Entertainment** : band (musicians), choir (singers), orchestra (musicians), troupe (actors/dancers), cast (actors), ensemble (musicians/dancers)

<details>
  <summary> <b> 4. Words(nouns) ending with <code>-s</code> is not necessarily plural </b> </summary>

<br/>

Examples: `news, mathematics, physics, economics, etc`

These words are grammatically singular in standard English.

```
The news is interesting.
Physics is interesting.
Mathematics is difficult.
```

**Words(nouns) ends with `-s` is not automatically plural.**


A word(noun) can look plural but behave singular.
```
The news is good.
Mathematics is important.
```

A word(noun) can look singular but refer to a group(plural).
```
The team is winning.
```

Conclusion : Don't determine just simply by looking for `s` but see the word behavior and context.
