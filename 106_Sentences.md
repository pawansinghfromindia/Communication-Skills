# Sentences 

<details>
  <summary> <b> Introduction - Sentences </b> </summary>

<br/>


> **A sentence is a group of words that expresses a complete thought.**

**Sentences always have a _subject_ and a _verb_.**

| Subject      | Verb         |  Sentence                 |
|--------------|--------------|---------------------------|
| I            | am working   | I am working.             |
| They         | are studying | They are studying.        |
| He/She       | is reading   | He/She is reading.        |
| The Children | are playing  | The children are playing. |

## Four Kinds of Sentence

<details>
  <summary> <b> 1. Declarative sentence </b> </summary>

<br/>

> A **declarative sentence** makes a **statement**. 

```
It is raining.
Rohit likes cricket.
The school bell was ringing.
The children are playing with the dog.
Shiva is in Banarasi.
```

Note: A declarative sentence ends with a **period** means fullstop.

</details>
<!----------------------------->

<details>
  <summary> <b> 2. Interrogative sentence </b> </summary>

<br/>

> An **interrogative sentence** asks a **question**. 

```
What is your name?
Where were you?
Why is the sky blue?
Who is talking to the teacher?
Are you happy?
```

Note: An interrogative sentence ends with a **question mark (?)** instead of a period.


</details>
<!----------------------------->

<details>
  <summary> <b> 3. Exclamatory sentence </b> </summary>

<br/>

> An **exclamatory sentence** makes a very strong statement called an **exclamation**. 

It shows a strong feeling/emotions such as surprise, excitement, sorrow or anger. 

```
How beautiful she is!
The silly boy!
What a heavy day!
```

Note: An exclamatory sentence ends with an **exclamation mark (!)** instead of a period.

</details>
<!----------------------------->

<details>
  <summary> <b> 4. Imperative sentence </b> </summary>

<br/>

> An **imperative sentence** gives an **order**.

```
Ask Rohan to come and see me.
Don't tell me lies.
Please leave.
Go to your room!
Speak up!
```

Note: An imperative sentence can end with an exclamation point (!) if the order is very firm otherwise 
generally ends with a period(fullstop). 

</details>

## Sentences

<details>
  <summary> <b> Simple Sentences </b> </summary>

<br/>

> A **clause** is a group of words that contains one subject and one verb

> A sentence that consists of one clause is called a **simple sentence**.

```
The girls are playing baseball.
Shreya found a good hiding place.
I am eating my breakfast.
Ram is wearing his new shoes today.
Will you help me?
The sky was very cloudy.
I can hear the birds.
Everyone was happy.
Is it raining again?
```

</details>
<!---------------------------->


<details>
  <summary> <b> Compound Sentences </b> </summary>

<br/>

> A **compound sentence** contains **two clauses** joined by a conjunction such as `and, or, but, so, etc`. 

Notice that there are two verbs, one on each side of the conjunction. 

```
Some people are always happy  and  some people are always sad.
<--------------------------->  +    <------------------------->
          v1 = are         conj = and           v2 = are

She opened the bag and took out a book.
<-----------------> +  <-------------->

Do you want coffee or would you prefer lemonade?
Is that a bird or is it a plane?

John is good at English but he's not very good at math.
Michael wants to see Star Wars but his friends have already seen it.

Ram dropped his sandwich so I gave him mine.
It started to rain so we went inside.
```

</details>
<!---------------------------->


<details>
  <summary> <b> Conditional Sentences </b> </summary>

<br/>

> To talk about things that are possible, we often use `if` in a sentence. So basically a sentence with `if` is called a **conditional sentence**. 

```
If it rains tomorrow, we shall not go to the beach.
<-------------------> <--------------------------->
  simple present         shall/will + infinitive
     tense verb
```

In the `if-clause`, use a verb in the **simple present tense**. <br/>
In the `main clause`, use `shall/will` and an **infinitive**.

```
If there's no rice in the cupboard, we will buy some more.
If we don't work hard, we will never learn.
If we leave now, we will arrive on time.
```

We can also put the `main clause` before the `if-clause`.
```
We will play indoors if it rains.
You will get sick if you don't eat good food.
Sohan will do well in his piano recital if he practices regularly.
```


</details>
<!---------------------------->


<details>
  <summary> <b> Positive Sentences and Negative Sentences </b> </summary>

<br/>

> **Positive sentences** tell you about something that exists or something that is happening. 

- A **negative sentence** doesn't contain the word `not` or any another negative word.

```
I like ice cream.
Manish is my brother.
The train leaves at five o'clock.
I'm feeling really tired.
She's finished her homework.
Dad is in the kitchen.
There's a cartoon on TV.
```

> **Negative sentences** tell you that something does not exist or is not happening.

- A **negative sentence** contains the word `not` or another negative word. 

```
I'm not very good at math.
Tarun isn't as tall as Alan.
We didn't hear you shout at us.
Meera hasn't read the Harry Potter books.
People can't see very well in the dark.
There's nothing interesting on TV tonight.
We've never been to China or Japan.
Nobody knows my secret.
There are no coins in my pocket.
```

</details>

## Questions

There are two kinds of questions: **Yes/No questions** and **WH questions**.

<details>
  <summary> <b> 1. Yes/No Questions </b> </summary>

<br/>

> When you ask a yes or no question, you want the answer **yes** or the answer **no**. 

Use the verbs **be (`is/am/are/was/were`)**, **have (`has/have/had`)** and **do (`do/does/did`)** along with **helping verbs** such as `can, will, should` when you ask these questions. 

Examples of Yes/No Question:
```
Que: Are you fine?
Ans: Yes
Que: Is he okay?
Ans: No
Que: Are we late?
Ans: No
Que: Is this your seat?
Ans: Yes
Que: Can you ride a bike?
Ans: No
Que: Don't you like pizza?
Ans: No
Que: May I sit here?
Ans: Yes
```

In questions, the **helping verb** comes **before** the subject. <br/>
The **other verb** comes **after** the subject. <br/>
The verb **be** also comes **before** the subject when it is an ordinary verb rather than a helping verb. 

| statement                       | question                      |
|---------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| `Dad is ill today.` <br/> `She has finished her homework.` <br/> `The cat doesn’t like noise.` <br/> `Mohan can ride a bike.` <br/> `Shreya could borrow your pencil.` <br/> `You may leave now.` <br/> `I think it will rain tomorrow.` <br/> `I saw Ram at the football game.` <br/> `Arijit sang a song.` <br/> `The computer needs to be repaired` | `Is Dad ill today?` <br/> `Has she finished her homework?` <br/> `Doesn't the cat like noise?` <br/> `Can Mohan ride a bike?` <br/> `Could Shreya borrow your pencil?` <br/> `May I leave now?` <br/> `Do you think it will rain tomorrow?` <br/> `Did you see Ram at the football game?` <br/> `Did Arijit sing a song?` <br/> `Does the computer need to be repaired?` |


</details>
<!---------------------------->

<details>
  <summary> <b> 2. WH Questions </b> </summary>

<br/>

> Use the question words `what, which, who (sometimes whom), whose, when, where, why, how` to ask for information.

- The verbs **be(`is/am/are/was/were`)**, **have(`has/have/had`)** and **do(`do/does/did`)**, and **helping verbs** such as `can, will,should` are also used in questions.

- The **helping verb** comes **before** the subject, as it does in yes or no questions. 

Examples
```
What is your name?
What date is it today?
Which boy is your brother?
Which house do you live in?
Who is the boy next to Alan?
Who (or Whom) did he ask?
Whose book is this?
When can I come to visit you?
When does the spring vacation start?
Where is the pencil that I left on my desk?
Where do the birds go when they fly away in winter?
How can Grandma read without her glasses?
How does a plane stay in the sky?
```

Sometimes the **WH-word** itself is the subject of the sentence. In this case, don't use **do** to form questions.
```
Who wants to come with me?
What caused the accident?
Which is the fastest car?
I’ve got my coat. Whose is this?
```

</details>

<details>
  <summary> <b> Question Tags </b> </summary>

<br/>

> **Sometimes people finish what they are saying with a short question**. <br/>
But Why do they do this? <br/>
Because they want **to know if the person they are speaking to agrees with them**. <br/> 
This short question is called a **Question tag**. 

```
The weather is lovely today, isn't it?
<-------------------------> <--------->
           +ve                  -ve
```
The main part of the sentence is positive, but the **question tag is negative**.

You expect the answer to a negative question tag to be yes.
```
The weather is lovely today, isn't it?
 Yes, it is.
```

- Use a helping verb and the subject of the sentence to make the **question tag**.

Notice that the subject has been replaced by a pronoun. <br/>
The pronoun in the question tag refers to the subject
```
Ram is older than you, isn't he?
Radha has got a dog, hasn't she?
Amit and I can go by train, can't we?
Rohan and Sohan should leave now, shouldn't they?
```

- If the main part of the sentence has **`I am`** in it, use `aren't I` in the question tag.
```
I'm your best friend, aren't I?
I'm taller than Sumiko, aren't I?
```

- If the main part of the sentence is negative, **the question tag is positive**.
You expect the answer to **a positive question tag** to be **no**. 
```
These questions aren't very difficult, are they?
You haven't read this book, have you?
Prakhar isn't as tall as I am, is he?
She isn't eight yet, is she?
There aren't many clouds in the sky, are there?
There isn't much wind today, is there?
There weren't any emails for me, were there?
```

</details>
