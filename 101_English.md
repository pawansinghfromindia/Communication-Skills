| **Control of Fire**                |           ~1 million+ years ago* | Humans learned to **control/use fire** for warmth, cooking, protection, and eventually better tools.                           |

  <summary> <b> History of Language </b> </summary>

<br/>
  
**How did languages come into existence?** <br/>
Ans : It is Debated by linguists, but ideas are;

1. **Gradual evolution theory** 
- Language likely evolved slowly in early humans (over tens of thousands of years) as a survival tool,
 for coordinating hunts, warning of danger, and building social bonds, alongside brain and vocal-tract development.

2. **Language families**  
- Most languages didn't appear independently. They evolved from common ancestor languages,
  then split as populations migrated and separated (like branches of a tree).
  
- English, Hindi, Spanish, Russian, and Persian are all distant cousins under the Indo-European family,
   believed to trace back to a language spoken roughly 6,000 years ago.

3. **No single "first language" But Sanskrit** 
- India is one of the oldest civilization also known as DevBhoomi.

### Subject and Predicate

In English Grammar, **Subject** and **Predicate** are the parts of sentences.

Every sentence is completed only by the formation of these two components. 

Example :
```

 Smoking is injurious to health.
<-------> <-------------------->
 Subject +    Predicate           == Sentence
```

> **Subject**

The subject of the sentence is the part that **_names the person or thing we are speaking about_**. 

> **Predicate** 

The predicate is the part of the sentence that **_tells something about the subject_**. 

Examples : Subject and Predicate in the sentence
```js
    Sentence      ==      Subject   +   Predicate                      
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I want a new book.	                         "I" + "want a new book"
Ram is taking the class.                     "Ram" + "is taking the class"
The umbrella is red.	                     "The umbrella" + "is red"
We ate pizza for dinner.	                 "We" + "ate pizza for dinner"
Students are learning English.	             "Students" "are learning English"
Rohit has a nice collection of marbles.      "Rohan" + "has a nice collection of marbles"
He/She is going to the temple.	             "He/She" +	"is going to the temple"
The child is waiting for his father.         "The Child" + "is waiting for his father"
The dog took the ball of thread. 	         "The dog" + "took the ball of thread"
The lion was hungry.                         "The lion" + "was hungry" 
Ram and Shyam have been selected.            "Ram and Shyam" +	"have been selected"
You and he should leave this place at once.	 "You and he" + "should leave this place at once"
The poor man had nothing to eat.	         "The poor man" + "had nothing to eat"
Life means fun.	                             "Life" +	"means fun"
Diamond cuts diamond.	                     "Diamond" + "cuts diamond"
Please give me some money.	                 "Please" + "give me some money"
```

> **Types of Subject**

The subject is the part of the sentence that talks about the noun which refers to the person or thing
doing the action in the form of verbs. 

There are three types of subject :

|   Types                 | Meaning	                                           |    Example                                                                   |
|-------------------------|----------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1. **Simple Subject**	  | Part of the sentence which has only subject	       | _Shreya_ is playing the guitar and singing at the party.                     |
| 2. **Complete Subject** | Part of the sentence has a subject with a modifier.|	_The old man_ took a deep breath _and then_ started the story.              |
| 3. **Compound Subject** | Part of the sentence has two or more subjects joined with the help of a conjunction | _Cricket and Kabaddi_ are my favorite sports.|


> **Types of Predicate**

The Predicate is the part of the sentence that tells something about the subject. 

There are three types of predicate :

| Types	                    | Meaning	                                                      | Example                                                                             |
|---------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1. **Simple Predicate**	  | Part of the sentence has only a verb.	                        | The dog _is running through the empty field_.                                       |
| 2. **Complete Predicate** | Part of the sentence has verbs with a modifier.	              | Mohan and his brother Rohan _never wanted costly gifts but preferred simple things._|
| 3. **Compound Predicate** |	Part of the sentence has two or more verbs with a conjunction.|	I _love the way_ he _plays the guitar, but his singing makes my ears bleed_.   |         |


> **Summary**

**Difference Between Subject and Predicate**

|                 Subject	                                          |          Predicate
|-------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Part of the sentence that tells "whom" or "what" the sentence is about|	Part of the sentence that tells what the subject in the sentence is doing |
| A subject tells "Who" or "What" in the sentence is all about 	    | The Predicate tells what the subject "is" or "does".                      |
| The subject can only be an object i.e. noun or pronoun	          | The predicate will contain verbs to tell what the subject is doing.       |


| Types of Subject                         | Types of Predicate                         |
|------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| - Simple Subject (only noun)             | - Simple Predicate (only verb)             |
| - Complete Subject( noun with a modifier)| - Complete Predicate (verb with a modifier)|
| - Compound Subject (two or more nouns joined with a conjunction) | - Compound Predicate (two or nouns joined with conjunction)|
