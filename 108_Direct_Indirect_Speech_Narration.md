<details>
  <summary> <b> 1. Introduction to Direct and Indirect Speech </b> </summary>


### 1. What is Direct Speech?

> Direct speech reports the exact words spoken by a person without changing those words.

> We put the spoken words inside quotation marks.

Example:
```
Ravi said, "I am happy."
- Reporting clause: Ravi said
- Exact spoken words: "I am happy."
- Quotation marks show the speaker's exact words.
```

```
SITUATION
A boy named Rahul speaks to his friend.

Rahul says, "I am happy."
The words inside quotation marks are Rahul's exact words.

Here is the sentence structure:
- Rahul says    --> the reporting clause.
- "I am happy." --> the reported speech.
- Quotation marks (" ") --> show the exact words spoken.
```
Remember: **in direct speech, we preserve the speaker's original words**.


### 2. What is Indirect Speech?

> **Indirect speech, also called reported speech**, reports what someone said without repeating their exact words.

Example:
```
Direct: Ravi said, "I am happy."
Indirect: Ravi said that he was happy.

Notice the changes:
- I changes to he bcuz Ravi is being discussed
- am changes to was bcuz tense changes
- Quotation marks are removed.
- that is introduced; it is often optional in statements.
- The reported words become part of the reporting sentence.
- The meaning remains the same, even though the sentence structure changes.
```
```
Direct:   Rahul says, "I am happy."
Indirect: Rahul says that he is happy.

Notice what changed:
- The word that is introduced; it is often optional in statements.
- The quotation marks are removed.
- The word I changes to he because Rahul is being discussed.
- The reported words become part of the reporting sentence.
- The meaning remains the same, even though the sentence structure changes.
```

### 3. **Direct vs. Indirect Speech**


| Feature            |	Direct speech	            | Indirect speech                      |
|--------------------|----------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Exact words	       | Yes	                      | Not necessarily                      |
| Quotation marks	   | Used	                      | Not used                             |
| Pronouns	         | Original speaker's words   |	May change                           |
| Tenses	           | Original tense is retained	| May change, depending on context     |
| Sentence structure | Quoted speech	            | Integrated into a reporting sentence |

Important: **tense changes are not automatic in every situation**.

```
Direct Speech:   Riya said, "I am tired."
- Riya's exact words are quoted. [Exact Words]
Indirect Speech: Riya said that she was tired.
- Riya's words are reported in the speaker's own sentence. [Reported meaning]
```

### 4. The three main things to remember

When converting direct speech into indirect speech, check these three things:

1. **Pronouns**: `I, we, you, he, she, they`.
2. **Tense**: `am -> was`, `will -> would`, `have -> had`, and so on.
3. **Time and place words**: `today -> that day`, `tomorrow -> the next day`, `here -> there`,
   when the context requires a change.

### 6. The basic idea 

We can explain it in three simple steps:
**1. Direct speech**: Write what the person said, using quotation marks. <br/>
**2. Indirect speech**: Report what the person said, without quotation marks. <br/>
**3. Preserve the meaning**: Change pronouns, tenses, or other words only as required by the context and the relevant grammar rules.

<br/>

Let's understand three terms: `reporting verb`, `reporting clause`, and `reported speech`.

`Rahul said to me, "I am busy."`

**1. Reporting clause**: This tells us who is speaking and to whom.
```
Rahul said to me
```

**2. Reporting verb**: This is the verb that introduces the speaker's words.
```
said
```

**3. Reported speech**: These are the actual words spoken by speaker.
```
"I am busy."
```

> Note: **The `reporting verb` is part of the `reporting clause`. It is not a separate, independent part of the sentence.**

### 1. What is a reporting verb?

> A reporting verb is the verb used to introduce or report someone's words, thoughts, questions, requests, or other communication.

Examples:
```
- said
- told
- asked
- replied
- explained
- requested
- ordered
- suggested
- exclaimed
```

Examples:
| Sentence	                                  | Reporting verb |
|---------------------------------------------|----------------|
| Rahul said, "I am ready."	                  | `said`         |
| Mother told me, "Be careful."               |	`told`         |
| She asked, "Where are you going?"           |	`asked`        |
| The teacher explained, "Plants need water." |	`explained`    | 
| He replied, "I don't know."	                | `replied`      |


### 3. What is a reporting clause?

> A reporting clause is the part of a sentence that identifies the speaker and contains the reporting verb. <br/>
> It may also contain an object or other information.

Examples:
| Sentence	                                                  | Reporting clause                 |
|-------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| **Ravi said** that he was tired.                            | `Ravi said`                      |
| **My mother told me** to study.                             | `My mother told me`              |
| **The teacher asked the students** whether they were ready. | `The teacher asked the students` |

Note: **The `reporting verb` is just the verb, whereas the `reporting clause` is the larger part containing that verb.**

```
Ravi said, "I am tired."
- Speaker: Ravi
- Reporting verb: said
- Reporting clause: Ravi said

My mother told me, "Study carefully."
- Speaker: My mother
- Reporting verb: told
- Object: me
- Reporting clause: My mother told me
```

### 4. What is reported speech?

> Reported speech is the part that contains the words or message being reported.

Examples:
| Direct speech	                       | Reported speech                         |
|--------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| Ravi said, "I am tired."	           | `Ravi said that he was tired.`          |
| She asked, "Where do you live?"	     | `She asked where I lived.`              |
| The teacher said, "Open your books." | `The teacher told us to open our books.`|

In **direct speech**, the reported speech is enclosed in **quotation marks**. <br/>
In **indirect speech**, it is incorporated into the reporting sentence **without quotation marks**.

Note: The reported speech doesn't always retain the exact original words. The message is reported according to the relevant grammar rules.

| Direct speech                              | Reporting verb  | Reporting clause   | Reported speech         |
|--------------------------------------------|-----------------|--------------------|-------------------------|
| `Priya said, "I am happy."`                | `said`          | `Priya said`       | `"I am happy."`         |
| `Aman told me, "You are late."`            | `told`          | `Aman told me`     | `"You are late."`       |

| `Mohan asked me whether I was ready.`      | ` asked`        | `Mohan asked me`   | `whether I was ready.`  |


> Common **reporting verbs include `said`, `told`, `asked`, `replied`, `explained`, and `shouted`.**

**Reporting clause**: Who reports the words and any related information, such as the listener.

**Reporting verb**: The verb that introduces the words.

**Reported speech**: The words being reported.

### 1. Pronouns

> A pronoun is a word used in place of a noun.

Example:
| Sentences                                             | Pronoun  |
|-------------------------------------------------------|----------|
| Rahul is a student. **He** studies every day.         |   `He`   |
| Priya is my friend. **She** is helpful.               |   `She`  |
| Rahul and Priya are friends. **They** study together. |   `They` |

In narration, pronouns may change because the original speaker's words are now being reported by someone else.

The key is to understand **who is speaking, who is listening, and who is reporting the speech**.

### 2. The three golden(main) rules of pronoun change

A traditional grammar rule helps you remember pronoun changes:

**1. First person - `I, we, me, us, my, our`** <br/>
Changes according to the subject of the reporting clause.

Example: 
| Direct Speech                   | Indirect Speech                      | Pronoun Changes |
|---------------------------------|--------------------------------------|-----------------|
| Ravi said, "I am ready."        | Ravi said that he was ready.         | `I` -> `he`     |
| Ram said, "I am happy."         | Ram said that he was happy.          | `I` -> `he`     |
| Sita said, "I love my mother."	| Sita said that she loved her mother. | `I` -> `she`    |
| The boys said, "We are ready."	| The boys said that they were ready.  | `we` -> `they`  |
| Ravi said, "My bag is new."	    | Ravi said that his bag was new.      | `my` -> `his`   |
| They said, "We are ready."      | They said that they were ready.      | `We` -> `they`  |
| Rita said, "My book is new."    | Rita said that her book was new.     | `My` -> `her`   |

**2. Second person - `you, your, yours`** <br/>
Changes according to the person being addressed.

Example: 
| Direct Speech                       | Indirect Speech                      | Pronoun Changes |
|-------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|-----------------|
| Ravi said to me, "You are helpful." | Ravi told me that I was helpful.     | `You` -> `I`    |
| Ram said to me, "You are kind."	    | Ram told me that I was kind.         | `You` -> `I`    |
| I said to Riya, "You are clever."	  | I told Riya that she was clever.     | `You` -> `She`  |
| The teacher said to the boys, "You are punctual."	| The teacher told the boys that they were punctual.| `you` -> `they` |
| Mother said to me, "Your room is clean." | Mother told me that my room was clean. | `Your` -> `my` |
| I said to Ravi, "You are right."    | I told Ravi that he was right.       | `You` -> `he`    |
| Ravi said to me, "You are late."    | Ravi told me that I was late.        | `You` -> `I`     |

We can notice that `you` does not always become `he` or `I`. It depends on whom the speaker addressed.

**3. Third person - `he, she, it, they, him, her, them, his, their`** <br/>
Usually remains unchanged, unless the context requires a different reference. 

Example: 
| Direct Speech                       | Indirect Speech                      | Pronoun Changes  |
|-------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|------------------|
| Ravi said, "She is intelligent."    | Ravi said that she was intelligent.  | `She` -> `she`   |
| Ram said, "He is honest."	          | Ram said that he was honest.         | `He`  -> `He`    |
| Sita said, "She is my friend."	    | Sita said that she was her friend.   | `She` -> `she`   |
| Mohan said, "They are playing."	    | Mohan said that they were playing.   | `They` -> `they` |
| Ram said, "I am tired."             | Ram said that he was tired.          | `I` -> `he`      |


### 3. A simple trick for exams

When you see direct speech, ask:
```
1. Who said these words?
2. Who was addressed?
3. Who is being talked about?
Answer these questions before changing the pronouns.
Then apply the tense rules
```

Let's solve
```
Direct: Amit said to Priya, "I will help you with your homework."

- Step 1: Identify the speaker.  Amit is speaking, so I becomes he.
- Step 2: Identify the listener. Amit is talking to Priya, so you becomes her and your becomes her.
- Step 3: Change the reporting structure and tense as required.

Indirect: Amit told Priya that he would help her with her homework.
```
We can notice that the **pronouns changed according to the people involved**, while **`will` changed to `would` under the usual backshift rule**.

### 4. Practice test

Convert these sentences into indirect speech.
```

Indirect: Priya said that she loved her family.
Why?
- I -> she because Priya is speaking.
- my -> her because the family belongs to Priya.

- will -> would

- my -> her because Neha is speaking.

Indirect: I said to Rohan that he was my best friend.
I told Rohan that he was my best friend.
Why?
- you -> he because Rohan was being addressed.
- my -> my because the original speaker is also the reporter.
- are -> was under the usual backshift rule.
```

### 5. Remember

| Direct speech     | Indirect speech rule                      |
|-------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| `I/we`            | Changes according to the speaker          | 
| `You/your`        | Changes according to the person addressed |
| `He/she/they`     | Usually remains the same referent         |
| `Will`            | Usually becomes `would`                   |
| `Have` completed  | Usually becomes `had` completed           | 
| `Said` to someone | Often becomes `told` someone              |
| `said` only       | often remains `said`                      |

**Notes**
> **First person: generally changes according to the speaker**.

> **Second person: generally changes according to the listener**.

> **Third person: generally remains unchanged**.

> Always identify the person to whom each pronoun refers before changing it.

<details>
  <summary> <b> 4. Rules for changing tenses </b> </summary>

<br/>

We will frequently be asked to change the `tense` when converting direct speech into indirect speech.

**When do we change the tense?**
```
                 Step 1: Find the reporting verb.
                                 |
                                 |
            Is it in the past tense, such as said or told?
                                  |
                                  | 
           Yes                                        No
            |                                          |
Usually backshift the tense.              Tense backshift may not be needed.
```
Example:
```
Past reporting verb:
Direct:   He said, "I am hungry."
Indirect: He said that he was hungry.

Present reporting verb:
Direct:   He says, "I am hungry."
Indirect: He says that he is hungry.
```

### 1. The golden rule: Tense Backshift

> **Tense backshift** means moving the tense of the reported speech one step back into the past when reporting someone's words, usually after a past reporting verb such as `said` or `told` or `asked`.


Example:
```
Direct: Ravi said, "I am busy."
- "am" =  present simple form of be.
Indirect: Ravi said that he was busy.
- Here, "am" -> "was".

Why does this happen?
- Bcuz the reporting verb said is in the past tense, and
the speaker's original words are being reported from a later viewpoint.
```

Note: Tense backshift is common, but it is not compulsory in every situation. We will see exceptions.

### 2. Complete(Master) tense conversion table

| Direct speech              | Indirect speech         | Example                                 |
|----------------------------|-------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| Present Simple             | Past Simple             | I work -> he worked                          |
| Present Continuous         | Past Continuous         | I am working -> he was working               |
| Present Perfect            | Past Perfect            | I have worked -> he had worked               |
| Present Perfect Continuous | Past Perfect Continuous | I have been working -> he had been working   |
| Past Simple                | Past Perfect            | I worked -> he had worked                    |
| Past Continuous            | Past Perfect Continuous | I was working -> he had been working         |
| Past Perfect               | Past Perfect            | I had worked -> he had worked                |
| Past Perfect Continuous    | Past Perfect Continuous | I had been working -> he had been working    |
| `Will + V1`                | `Would + V1`            | I will go -> he would go                     |
| `Will be + V-ing`          | `Would be + V-ing`      | I will be going -> he would be going         |
| `Will have + V3`           | `Would have + V3`       | I will have gone -> he would have gone       |

> **V1 = base verb, V3 = past participle.**

The key idea is that the **past perfect and past perfect continuous usually remain unchanged** because they are already past-perfect forms.

Indirect: She said that she played cricket.

```

Indirect: He said that he was studying.

```

| Past tenses    |
|----------------|

**Past Simple -> Past Perfect**
```
Direct:   Ravi said, "I bought a car."
Indirect: Ravi said that he had bought a car.

Direct:   Priya said, "I completed my work."
Indirect: Priya said that she had completed her work.
```
**Past Continuous -> Past Perfect Continuous**
```
Direct:   She said, "I was cooking."
Indirect: She said that she had been cooking.

```
Direct:   He said, "I had completed the task."

| Future tenses  |
|----------------|

**What happens to future tense?** <br/>
Future forms also change when backshift is required.

| Direct speech	                              | Indirect speech                                         |
|---------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
| `will` -> `would`	                          | "I will go." -> He said he would go.                    |
| `shall` -> `would`(often)	                  | "I shall return." -> He said he would return.           |
| `am/is/are going to` -> `was/were going to` | "I am going to study." -> He said he was going to study.|


**`will` -> `would`**
```
Direct:   Aman said, "I will call you."
Indirect: Aman said that he would call me.

Direct:   Anil said, "I will help you."
Indirect: Anil said that he would help me.
```

**`will be + V-ing` -> `would be + V-ing`**
```
Direct:   She said, "I will be travelling tomorrow."
Indirect: She said that she would be travelling the next day.
```

**`will have + V3`** -> **`would have + V3`**
```
Direct: He said, "I will have finished the work."
Indirect: He said that he would have finished the work.
```

| Modal Verbs   |
|---------------|

**What happens to modal verbs?** <br/>
Modal verbs express ability, permission, possibility, obligation, and other meanings.

| Example                               | Direct speech   -> Indirect speech                     |
|---------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| `can` -> `could`                      | "I can swim."  -> He said he could swim.               |
| `may` -> `might`                      | "I may come." -> He said he might come.                |
| `must` -> `had to` (when obligation is intended) | "I must leave." -> He said he had to leave. |
| `will` -> `would`                     | "I will call." -> He said he would call.               |
| `shall` -> `should/would`, depending on meaning | Context matters.                             |

**Some modal verbs generally remain unchanged: `should, would, could, might, ought to`**
```
Direct:   She said, "I could solve it."
Indirect: She said that she could solve it.

```

### 4. Important exceptions

Tense does not always have to change.

**A. Reporting verb in the present**
```
Direct: He says, "I am tired."
Indirect: He says that he is tired.
```
Because `says` is present tense, the usual backshift does not apply.

**B. Universal truths and scientific facts**
```
Direct:   The teacher said, "The Earth revolves around the Sun."
Indirect: The teacher said that the Earth revolves around the Sun.
```
The present tense is retained because this is a universal fact.

**C. A fact that is still true**
```
Direct: Maya said, "I live in Delhi."
- If Maya still lives in Delhi, we can say:
Indirect: Maya said that she lives in Delhi.
```
If you are simply following the standard backshift pattern in an exam, `lived` is usually expected.

<br/>

We'll learn how words such as `today, tomorrow, here, this, and these` may change when we report someone’s words.

| Direct speech  | Indirect speech    |
|----------------|--------------------|
| `today`        | `that day`         |

### 1. Time expressions

When someone speaks, they use time expressions from their own point of view. <br/>
When we report those words later, the time expressions may need to change.

| Direct speech  | Indirect speech     |
|----------------|---------------------|
| `now`          | `then`              |
| `today`        | `that day`          |
| `yesterday`    | `the previous day`  |
| `tomorrow`     | `the next day`      |
| `last night`   | `the previous night`|
| `next week`    | `the following week`|
| `ago`          | `before`            |
| `last week`    | `the previous week` |
| `last month`	 | `the previous month`        |
| `next month`	 | `the following month`       |
| `a year ago`	 | `a year before`             |
| `tonight`      | `that night`                |
| `yesterday morning` |	`the previous morning` |

The best choice can vary with the precise time reference and context.

Examples:
```
Direct:   Rahul said, "I am busy today."
Indirect: Rahul said that he was busy that day.

Direct:   Neha said, "I will visit you tomorrow."
Indirect: Neha said that she would visit me the next day.

Direct:   Amit said, "I met him two days ago."
Indirect: Amit said that he had met him two days before.
```

### 2. Place expressions

Words that describe location can change when the speaker's original location differs from the reporter's location.

| Direct speech	| Indirect speech |
|---------------|-----------------|
| `here`       	| `there`         |
| `this place`  |	`that place`    |
| `come here`   |	`go there` (when the perspective requires it) |

Examples:
```
Direct:   Riya said, "I live here."
Indirect: Riya said that she lived there.

Direct:   The teacher said, "Come here."
Indirect: The teacher told the student to go there.
```

### 3. Demonstrative words

Demonstratives help us point to people or things.

| Direct speech	| Indirect speech |
|---------------|-----------------|
| `This`        | `That`          |
| `These`       | `Those`         |

Examples:
```
Direct:   He said, "This apple is sweet."
Indirect: He said that that apple was sweet.

Direct:   She said, "These books are mine."
Indirect: She said that those books were hers.
```
However, if the same object is still near the reporter, this or these may remain appropriate.

### 4. Example

```
Direct speech:   Priya said, "I will finish this work here tomorrow."
Indirect speech: Priya said that she would finish that work there the next day.
```
| Element	      | Direct	      | Indirect        |
|---------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Pronoun	      | `I`	          | `she`           |
| Tense/modal	  | `will` finish |	`would` finish  |
| Demonstrative	| `this` work	  | `that` work     |
| Place	        | `here`	      | `there`         |
| Time	        | `tomorrow`	  | `the next day`  |

### 5. When should these words NOT change?

The changes above are not mechanical rules that apply in every situation.
```
Direct:   Rahul said yesterday, "I will finish this work tomorrow."
Indirect: Rahul said yesterday that he would finish this work tomorrow.
- Here, tomorrow may remain tomorrow because the reporting happens on the same day as Rahul's original statement.
```
If you report this on the same day and the original work is still the same work.

So, before changing a time, place, or demonstrative word, ask:
```
1. When was the original statement made?
2. When am I reporting it?
3. Am I in the same place?
4. Am I referring to the same object or situation?
```
This helps you produce meaningful sentences rather than simply memorizing substitutions.

### 6. Practice test

```
1. He said, "I will come tomorrow."
- He said that he would come the next day.
- Will changes to would, tomorrow usually changes to the next day, and I changes to he.

2. She said, "I saw him yesterday."
- She said that she had seen him the previous day.
- Yesterday usually becomes the previous day; saw usually changes to had seen.

3. Ravi said, "These flowers are beautiful."
- Ravi said that those flowers were beautiful.
- Those is the conventional conversion of these, and are usually changes to were after a past reporting verb.

4. She said, "I live here."
- She said that she lived there.
- Here commonly changes to there when the reporting location differs.

5. He said, "I met her two days ago."
- He said that he had met her two days before.
- Ago usually becomes before in a past reporting context.

6. Must "tomorrow" always change to "the next day"?
- No, it depends on when the statement is reported.
- If the reporting occurs on the same day and the time reference is unchanged, tomorrow may remain tomorrow.
```

### 7. Quick revision

- **Time**: `today -> that day`; `tomorrow -> the next day`; `yesterday -> the previous day`.
- **Place**: `here -> there`.
- **Demonstratives**: `this -> that`; `these -> those`.

Remember: changes depend on the reporting time, location, and context.

### 1. What is a statement?

> A statement is a sentence that gives information or expresses an idea, fact, opinion, or feeling.

Examples:
```
Rohan is a good student.
Priya likes mangoes.
The teacher is explaining the lesson.
They have completed their homework.
```
When these sentences are spoken and reported, we can express them in direct or indirect speech.

```
Direct speech:   Rohan said, "I am a good student."
Indirect speech: Rohan said that he was a good student.
```
Notice that the sentence is a statement, not a question, command, or exclamation.

### 2. Rule 1: Statements with said

When the reporting clause uses said and there is no object after it, the basic pattern is:

**Structure** <br/>
**Direct speech: `Subject + said + "statement"`** <br/>
**Indirect speech: `Subject + said + (that) + reported statement`**

Examples
```
Present simple
Direct: Amit said, "I play cricket."
Indirect: Amit said that he played cricket.
Changes:
- I -> he
- play -> played
- Quotation marks are removed.
- That is introduced but is optional.

Present continuous
Direct: Sita said, "I am reading a book."
Indirect: Sita said that she was reading a book.
Changes:
- I -> she
- am reading -> was reading

Future tense
Direct: Rahul said, "I will help you."
Indirect: Rahul said that he would help me.
Here, you becomes me only if the original listener was the person reporting the sentence.
```
Tip: Never change `you` automatically to `me`. First identify the person being addressed.
