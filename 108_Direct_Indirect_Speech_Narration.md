<details>
  <summary> <b> 1. Introduction to Direct and Indirect Speech </b> </summary>


### 1. What is Direct Speech?
> Direct speech reports someone's exact words. We put the spoken words inside quotation marks.

Example:
```
Ravi said, "I am happy."
- Reporting clause: Ravi said
- Exact spoken words: "I am happy."
- Quotation marks show the speaker's exact words.
```

### 2. What is Indirect Speech?

> Indirect speech reports what someone said without repeating their exact words.

Example:
```
Direct: Ravi said, "I am happy."
Indirect: Ravi said that he was happy.

Notice the changes:
- I changes to he.
- am changes to was.
- Quotation marks are removed.
- that is added; it can sometimes be omitted.
```

**Direct vs. Indirect Speech**

| **Direct Speech**         | **Indirect Speech**            |
|---------------------------|--------------------------------|
| `She said, "I am tired."` | `She said that she was tired.` |
| Exact words               |   Reported meaning             |


### 3. The three main things to remember

When converting direct speech into indirect speech, check these three things:

1. **Pronouns**: `I, we, you, he, she, they`.
2. **Tense**: `am -> was`, `will -> would`, `have -> had`, and so on.
3. **Time and place words**: `today -> that day`, `tomorrow -> the next day`, `here -> there`,
   when the context requires a change.

### 4. Quick practice

Convert these sentences into indirect speech.
```
Ravi said, "I am busy."
Ravi said that he was busy.

She said, "I like tea."
She said that she liked tea.

Aman said, "I will help you."
Aman said that he would help me.
```

<details>
  <summary> <b> 2. Parts of a reported sentence: reporting verb, reporting clause, and reported speech </b> </summary>

<br/>

Pronoun changes are one of the most important rules in narration. <br/>
The key is to understand **who is speaking, who is listening, and who is reporting the speech**.

### 1. The three golden rules

A traditional grammar rule helps you remember pronoun changes:

1. First person - `I, we, me, us` <br/>
Changes according to the subject of the reporting clause.

Example: 
```
Ravi said, "I am ready."
Ravi said that he was ready.
```

2. Second person - `you, your` <br/>
Changes according to the person being addressed.

Example: 
```
Ravi said to me, "You are helpful."
Ravi told me that I was helpful.
```

3. Third person - `he, she, it, they` <br/>
Usually remains unchanged, unless the context requires a different reference. 

Example: 
```
Ravi said, "She is intelligent."
Ravi said that she was intelligent.
```

### 2. Learn through examples

| Direct speech                    | Indirect speech                  |
|----------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Ram said, "I am tired."          | Ram said that he was tired.      |
| Rita said, "My book is new."     | Rita said that her book was new. |
| They said, "We are ready."       | They said that they were ready.  |
| Ravi said to me, "You are late." | Ravi told me that I was late.    |
| I said to Ravi, "You are right." | I told Ravi that he was right.   |

We can notice that `you` does not always become `he` or `I`. It depends on whom the speaker addressed.

### 3. A simple trick for exams

When you see direct speech, ask:
```
1. Who said these words?
2. Who was addressed?
3. Who is being talked about?
Answer these questions before changing the pronouns.
Then apply the tense rules from Topic 1.
```

### 4. Practice test

Convert these sentences into indirect speech.
```
Direct:   Priya said, "I love my family."
Indirect: Priya said that she loved her family.
Why?
- I -> she because Priya is speaking.
- my -> her because the family belongs to Priya.
- love -> loved because the reporting verb said is in the past.

Direct:   Amit said to me, "You have completed your work."
Indirect: Amit said to me that I had completed my work.
Amit told me that I had completed my work.
Why?
- you -> I because Amit was speaking to you.
- your -> my because the work belongs to the person addressed.
- have completed -> had completed under the usual backshift rule.

Direct:   The boys said, "We will help you."
Indirect: The boys said that they would help me.
- We -> they
- will -> would
- you -> me (given the stated context)

Direct:   Neha said, "He is my brother."
Indirect: Neha said that he was her brother.
Why?
- he remains he because it refers to another male person.
- my -> her because Neha is speaking.
- is -> was under the usual backshift rule.

Direct:   I said to Rohan, "You are my best friend."
Indirect: I said to Rohan that he was my best friend.
I told Rohan that he was my best friend.
Why?
- you -> he because Rohan was being addressed.
- my -> my because the original speaker is also the reporter.
- are -> was under the usual backshift rule.
```

### 5. Remember

| Direct speech     | Indirect speech rule                      |
|-------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| `I/we`            | Changes according to the speaker          | 
| `You/your`        | Changes according to the person addressed |
| `He/she/they`     | Usually remains the same referent         |
| `Will`            | Usually becomes `would`                   |
| `Have` completed  | Usually becomes `had` completed           | 
| `Said` to someone | Often becomes `told` someone              |
| `said` only       | often remains `said`                      |
